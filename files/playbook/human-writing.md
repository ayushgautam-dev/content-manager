# Write like a person, not like AI

The writer's default style, used until the person has their own voice notes. Once
`/people/<handle>/memory/voice.md` exists, it wins on style. It never wins over
the honesty rules in `craft.md` or over `sector-rules.md`.

Adapted for LinkedIn posts from the Wikipedia editors' guide
[*Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
by its contributors, licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. This file is shared under the same
licence (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The guide catalogues
patterns that make text read as machine-written; below they are turned around
into rules for writing that does not.

## What a reader notices first

People scrolling LinkedIn decide in a line or two whether a post was written by
someone who knows the subject. They stop trusting a post when it sounds like it
could be about anything.

1. **Say the thing.** "The fine was $4.2 million" beats "The penalty underscores
   the evolving regulatory landscape." Lead with the fact, the number, the name.
2. **Use "is", "has", "was".** Not "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features",
   "offers" when you mean "is" or "has".
3. **Do not inflate.** No claims that something "marks a pivotal moment",
   "reflects broader trends", "highlights the importance of", "plays a vital
   role". If it matters, show the consequence instead.
4. **No vague crowds.** Not "experts say", "many believe", "industry observers
   note". Name who said it and link it, or say it as your own view.
5. **No "not just X, but Y".** Avoid the reflex contrasts: "It's not about X,
   it's about Y", "This isn't X. It's Y.", "Y rather than X". Once in a post, if
   it is the actual point, is the limit.
6. **Break the rule of three.** Machine text loves three adjectives and three
   parallel phrases. Use the number of items the material has. Two is fine. One is
   often better.
7. **Go easy on the em dash.** At most one in a post; use a full stop or a comma.
8. **No staged one-liners.** Pairs like "Agents did the typing. A person owned
   the loop." or "Impressive. Also not the point." read as performance. Say the
   point once, plainly.
9. **No tidy moral at the end.** Skip "Ultimately…", "In the end…", "The future
   of X is bright", "challenges remain, but…". End on the real question or the
   concrete takeaway.

## Words that give it away

Avoid these unless they are the plainest word for the job, and never several in
one post: *additionally* (to open a sentence), *align with*, *boasts*,
*bolstered*, *crucial*, *deep dive*, *delve*, *emphasizing*, *enduring*,
*enhance*, *fostering*, *garner*, *highlight* (as a verb), *interplay*,
*intricate*, *key* (as an adjective), *landscape* (as an abstract noun),
*meticulous*, *pivotal*, *robust*, *showcase*, *tapestry*, *testament*,
*underscore*, *vibrant*, *seamless*, *game-changer*, *unlock*, *elevate*,
*navigate* (anything that is not a road), *in today's fast-paced world*,
*here's the thing*, *let that sink in*, *thrilled to announce*.

## Formatting that gives it away

- **No bold headings inside a post** ("**Key takeaway:**"). LinkedIn posts are
  paragraphs, not documents.
- **No bullet list where each item starts with a bolded label and a colon.**
- **No emoji as bullets** (✅ 🚀 💡 👉) unless the person's voice notes say they
  use them. Default: none.
- **No Title Case Headlines** in the body.
- **No markdown** (asterisks, pound signs): LinkedIn shows them literally.
- **Straight quotes and plain dashes** are fine; do not decorate.

## What makes it sound like a person

- **A specific moment.** "Last Tuesday a customer asked…" beats a general truth.
- **An opinion with a reason.** "I think this rule is wrong, because…"
- **Uneven rhythm.** Some short sentences. Some longer ones that carry a thought
  through. Not every paragraph the same length.
- **Ordinary words.** The words the person would use out loud to a colleague.
- **Admitting what you do not know.** "We have not tested this ourselves" is
  stronger than a confident guess.
- **Cut the first and last sentence of the draft.** They are usually throat
  clearing. Check the post still works without them.

## Before you finish

Read the draft once looking only for the patterns above. Rewrite every sentence
that would fit in a post about a different topic.
