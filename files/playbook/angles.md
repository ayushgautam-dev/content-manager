# Angles: what's worth posting, and where it comes from

Nobody wants a summary. People follow a **point of view**: on something new that
changes what the reader should think or do, on a story everyone in their field is
talking about this week, on what doing the work taught the person. A source or a
news story is evidence for the point of view, never the post itself.

Every morning the scout turns the person's sources and the news into a **stream**
of about five ideas (*sparks* in the tables). A spark is an angle (one sentence
that could be the whole post), a first line, why now, and the evidence.
"Write this post" hands it to the writer.

## The three streams

| Stream | Comes from | Share of a good week |
|---|---|---|
| **Sources**: something new, decoded | `source_items` the screener kept: studies, launches, rulings, filings, whatever the person asked for | ~35% |
| **World**: a take on what's happening | `trends` from the news scan | ~40% |
| **You**: who the person is | their beliefs, stories, work and past posts (`about.md`, `strategy.md`) | ~25% |

Skip a stream on a day it has nothing honest to offer. Three strong ideas beat
five weak ones. Until the person has voice notes, the **you** stream leans only on
what the website and their onboarding answers say; keep it to one idea at most.

## Turning material into an angle

**Sources → what it changes.** Never "a new X came out" and stop. Find the way in
the reader cares about:
- the number that contradicts what the reader assumes;
- the small, practical version of it ("one setting", "a five-minute check");
- what everyone else will miss about it;
- what it means for the person's audience specifically (their market, country,
  role, budget);
- what the person's company already does that it supports, said as practice,
  never as a pitch.
Use only what the item row supports (`headline`, `summary`, `why_it_matters`,
`limits_note`, `item_type`, `quality_score`). Carry the limit into the angle.
Prefer `quality_score` of 7 or more (0–10); never use a rejected item.

**World → take.** Never summarise the news. Say what it means from where the
person sits:
- "Everyone is reading X as A. From where we sit, it looks like B."
- "The part of X nobody is talking about is …"
- "X is a good move, and here is what is missing around it."
- a company, regulator or market move, and what it says about where the field is
  heading.

**You → evergreen.** Beliefs the person repeats, their own history, a customer
story (anonymous unless cleared in `about.md`), how the team works, a hiring post.
These come from what the person has said, written or lived, never invented.

## A good spark

- **Angle**: one sentence a stranger would argue with or save.
- **Hook**: the first line as the person would write it.
- **Why now**: today's reason (a story's heat, something published this week, a
  date). Evergreen sparks say "evergreen".
- **Evidence**: the links behind it.
- **Format**: `image` for most; `carousel` for frameworks, checklists and things
  walked through step by step; `text` rarely (see craft.md).
- **Image idea**: whose headline or title goes on the source card, and a
  three-line caption; for a carousel, the slides in one line each.
- **Fresh until**: world sparks 2–4 days; source sparks about two weeks; evergreen
  none.

## Honesty

All of craft.md applies to sparks exactly as it applies to posts, and so do the
sector rules when they exist.
